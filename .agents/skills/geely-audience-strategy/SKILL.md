# Geely EX2 Landing Page — Audience & Content Strategy

Style-agnostic. This skill governs **what information appears, in what
order, and how it's worded** — never colors, fonts, or layout mechanics.
Pair it with whichever visual design skill or style is used for the page;
the content rules below hold regardless.

## Who is landing on this page

- 25–45+ years old, owns or is about to buy a Geely EX2.
- Financially capable — this is a discretionary upgrade for a car they
  already invested real money in, not a budget hack.
- Not an IT/Android person. Doesn't know what ADB, APK, root, or
  firmware version really means, and doesn't want to learn — they
  want to know what they get and whether it's safe for their car.
- Landed here either already knowing they want this service (searched
  for it, saw an ad, heard from a friend) or is comparing 2–3 similar
  offers. Either way, they decide fast — attention span for a wall of
  text is low. If the value isn't obvious in 5–10 seconds of scanning,
  they leave.
- Cares about: is this safe / reversible / won't void anything, does
  it actually work on MY exact trim, how much does it cost, how long
  does it take, can I trust this person/shop.

## The core rule: front-load the decision-relevant facts

This audience does not read a page top to bottom looking for buried
value — they scan for the 4–5 facts that let them decide, then either
convert or leave. Every section should assume the visitor might stop
reading after it. That means:

1. **The offer and the outcome go in the hero — no exceptions.** What
   do they get (in car-owner language, not technical language) and
   what will their head unit actually be able to do afterward. Never
   open with a technical explanation of the process.
2. **Price/starting price visible early**, or at minimum a clear
   "packages from X" — this audience distrusts pages that hide pricing
   until a form submission; it reads as a sales trap to an older buyer.
3. **Compatibility/requirements stated plainly and early** (which trim,
   which firmware version) — this is the single fact most likely to
   make or break relevance, so don't bury it after marketing copy.
4. **Reversibility & safety reassurance near the top**, not just in an
   FAQ at the bottom — "does this void my warranty / can it be undone /
   is this safe for the car" is usually the #1 silent objection for
   this age group with an expensive car, and if unaddressed early they
   simply won't scroll further to find the reassurance.

## Language rules

- Zero unexplained jargon in visible copy: no "ADB", "APK", "root",
  "SELinux", "flash", "firmware patch" as user-facing terms. Translate
  every technical step into its outcome: not "we install a custom
  launcher via ADB" but "we replace the slow default menu with a fast,
  simple one."
- Short sentences, concrete nouns. Avoid abstract marketing language
  ("elevate your driving experience") in favor of specific, checkable
  claims ("opens 2x faster", "works with Spotify and Yandex Music").
- Address the reader as a car owner making a practical choice, not as
  a tech enthusiast — tone is closer to a trusted mechanic explaining
  an upgrade than a software product pitch.
- Numbers over adjectives wherever possible: a stated turnaround time,
  a stated price, a stated list of supported apps beats "fast" and
  "many apps."

## Trust signals this audience specifically needs

Older, non-technical buyers are more risk-averse about handing their
car's electronics to a third party than a younger DIY audience would
be. Make sure the page surfaces, explicitly and not just implied:

- Who is doing the work (a name/face/shop, not an anonymous "we").
- Before/after proof — visual or described — of the actual result on
  a real EX2, not generic stock renders.
- A plain answer to "what if something goes wrong" (reversibility,
  guarantee, support after the fact).
- Real contact method (phone/messenger), not only a contact form —
  this age group wants the option to just call and ask.

## Section order (content priority, independent of visual layout)

1. Hero: outcome + who it's for (Geely EX2) + starting price or "see
   pricing" + one clear CTA.
2. What changes for you (in outcomes, not steps) — 3–5 concrete
   benefits.
3. Compatibility/requirements — which trim, which firmware, checked
   plainly.
4. Before/after proof.
5. Safety/reversibility/guarantee — explicit reassurance section, not
   left to the FAQ only.
6. How it works — simple 3-step process, still in plain language.
7. Pricing/packages.
8. Contact — multiple channels, prominent.
9. FAQ — for the remaining, less critical questions only; the top
   objections should already be answered above this point.

## What to avoid

- Don't lead with the technical process or the modder's own excitement
  about the tech — lead with the buyer's outcome.
- Don't hide price, requirements, or safety info behind a "contact us
  to learn more" wall — for this audience that reads as evasive, not
  as lead-gen strategy.
- Don't assume the visitor will scroll far enough to reach reassurance
  or proof placed only near the bottom — put the highest-anxiety
  answers (safety, compatibility, cost) in the first two screens.
